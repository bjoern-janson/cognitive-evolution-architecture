# CEA Epistemic Architecture — Conception

## Purpose

CEA contains two architectures that must remain distinct:

```text
Computational architecture ≠ Epistemic architecture
```

The computational architecture proposes mechanisms by which capability may accumulate.

The epistemic architecture governs what evidence is required before the project is entitled to claim that capability accumulated.

The master law is:

> **Capability may accumulate; authority must be earned at every transition.**

A stronger equivalent is:

```text
A precursor cannot manufacture the authority of its successor.
```

## Three Authority Boundaries

CEA currently recognizes three major authority firewalls.

### 1. Ingestion

```text
conceptual compatibility ↛ evidential authority
semantic resonance ↛ truth
```

A source, paper, transcript, analogy, or external theory may be highly relevant to a CEA hypothesis without providing a witness for that hypothesis.

The ingestion path is:

```text
SourceClaim
→ source verification
→ VerifiedPhenomenon
→ CEA relevance
→ discriminating experiment
→ Witness
```

No stage may be skipped.

### 2. Composition

```text
ComponentWitness ↛ CompositionWitness
```

Even if every component hypothesis independently survives its own experiment, the proposed interaction remains a new hypothesis.

```text
Witness[A] ∧ Witness[B] ∧ ...
↛
CompositionWitness
```

The composition must survive its own experiment, including negative controls and ablations capable of distinguishing genuine interaction from additive gains.

### 3. Execution

Issue #44 supplies the runtime analogue:

```text
ValidatedEvaluation ↛ MutationOccurred
```

Evaluation may justify minting authority, but mutation still depends on capability validity against live commit-time state.

Thus scientific reasoning and runtime state share a common conservation pattern:

```text
having a precursor ≠ being entitled to perform the successor transition
```

## Methodological Kernel

The current CEA methodological kernel is:

```text
K1: Specification ≠ Implementation ≠ Verification Result
K2: Conceptual compatibility ↛ authority
K3: Component witness ↛ composition witness
K4: Evaluation ↛ mutation
```

These rules prevent three characteristic authority leaks:

- **analogy inflation** — resemblance silently becomes evidence;
- **compositional inflation** — component success silently becomes architecture success;
- **operational inflation** — a favorable evaluation silently becomes committed state.

## Authority Conservation

CEA generalizes the Issue #44 architectural law into a scientific-reasoning constraint:

```text
No downstream research object may manufacture authority absent upstream.
```

This does not mean every successor must be weaker than its predecessor. Experiments can legitimately create new evidence. It means the increase in authority must be caused by a discriminating transition that is entitled to produce it.

The relevant form is therefore:

```text
Authority_{t+1} > Authority_t
only if
Transition_t supplied new identifying evidence.
```

Evidence may increase authority only along dimensions it can identify.

## Evidential Hierarchy

```text
Source
→ Phenomenon
→ Hypothesis
→ Experiment
→ Witness
→ Composition
→ Composition Experiment
→ Composition Witness
```

Each transition carries a distinct burden of proof.

A source claim does not establish the phenomenon it reports.

A verified phenomenon does not establish the mechanism that generated it.

A hypothesis does not establish its own discriminating test.

A positive experiment does not become a witness unless the result actually discriminates the proposition from relevant alternatives.

A set of component witnesses does not establish the interaction claim made by their composition.

## Authority Gradient

```text
interesting < relevant < verified < discriminating < witnessed
```

These should be understood as different epistemic capabilities rather than generic confidence labels.

- **Interesting** — worthy of attention; no evidential authority implied.
- **Relevant** — bears on a defined hypothesis or distinction; no verification implied.
- **Verified** — occurrence or source claim independently established under stated conditions; mechanism may remain unknown.
- **Discriminating** — evidence distinguishes among competing explanations or predictions.
- **Witnessed** — a proposition has survived its defined discriminating experiment within the tested scope.

No category inherits authority outside the dimensions established by its evidence.

## Protected Distinctions

CEA currently protects:

```text
SourceClaim ≠ VerifiedPhenomenon
VerifiedPhenomenon ≠ Mechanism
Hypothesis ≠ Experiment
Experiment ≠ Witness
ComponentWitness ≠ CompositionWitness
InheritedCompetence ≠ PersistentLearning ≠ OpenEndedImprovement
Specification ≠ Implementation ≠ VerificationResult
Evaluation ≠ Mutation
```

These distinctions should remain reopenable when new evidence exposes a better decomposition, but they may not be collapsed for convenience.

## Scientific / Cognitive Isomorphism

CEA currently observes a useful structural analogy:

```text
Scientific process:
Source → Phenomenon → Hypothesis → Witness

Cognitive process:
Observation → Evaluation → Capability → Mutation
```

The analogy is methodological, not evidence that the two processes are identical.

In both cases, the controlling rule is:

```text
precursor possession ≠ successor authority
```

This is the conceptual bridge between CEA's research methodology and the Issue #44 authority substrate.

## Status

This document is a **conception baseline** for CEA's epistemic architecture. It describes how research claims are allowed to acquire authority. It is not evidence that the CEA computational architecture is correct.
